Every family thinks recruiting is a mystery until someone explains it plainly. It isn't. It's a process — and like any process, it rewards people who understand how it works and consistently do the right things, and it punishes people who wait around hoping to be discovered.

## How Coaches Actually Find Players

There are three real pipelines coaches use to find players, and they're not equal.

**Film first.** Before a coach ever sees you in person, they've usually seen film — either sent directly by a player, shared by a high school or AAU coach, or found through a recruiting service. Film is the first filter. If it doesn't hold up, nothing else matters.

**Events they already attend.** Coaches have limited recruiting budgets and travel to a fixed number of events each live period. If your team or your circuit isn't somewhere a coach already plans to be, you're invisible to them no matter how well you play.

**Direct communication.** This is the piece most players skip. A short, specific email or direct message that shows you've done research on the program — not a mass copy-paste — puts you on a coach's radar in a way that talent alone doesn't.

## Recruiting Is a Two-Way Process

Here's the mental shift that changes everything: you are not waiting to be picked. You are actively building relationships with programs that make sense for you, and giving coaches a reason to notice you.

That means:

- Building a basic highlight and full-game film library early
- Researching programs that fit your level, not just your dream list
- Sending direct, specific outreach — not generic mass emails
- Following up. Most players email once and never follow up again.

## Fit Beats Rankings

The single most common mistake I see: families obsess over rankings and offers from schools that aren't a realistic or good fit, while ignoring programs where a player could actually play significant minutes and develop.

A rotation spot at a program where you're wanted, coached well, and given real opportunity will do more for a player's development and long-term options than a bench role at a "bigger name" school. Rankings measure hype. They don't measure fit.

## The Realistic Timeline

Recruiting conversations for most players — outside of a small pool of elite, nationally ranked prospects — really start picking up sophomore and junior year. That doesn't mean freshman year doesn't matter. It's when the groundwork gets built: film, grades, target levels, and understanding how the process works.

Waiting until senior year to start is the single most common and costly mistake families make.

## The Verdict

Recruiting isn't luck, and it isn't only about talent. It's a process that rewards players and families who understand how coaches actually operate, communicate proactively, and target the right level instead of chasing rankings. Start the groundwork early, be specific and consistent in outreach, and prioritize fit over name recognition.
