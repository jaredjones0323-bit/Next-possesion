Every summer, families make the same mistake: chasing the AAU team with the biggest name instead of the team that will actually get their player in front of coaches, in real minutes, against real competition.

## Exposure Comes From Playing, Not From a Jersey

College coaches at live events are evaluating players in game situations — decision-making under pressure, defense, how a player communicates, how they respond to a bad stretch. None of that is visible from the bench.

A star player on a lesser-known regional team who plays 32 minutes a game against solid competition will get more real evaluation than a role player getting spot minutes for a nationally recognized program. Name recognition of the team doesn't transfer to the player who isn't on the floor.

## Questions to Ask Before Committing to a Team

Before joining any AAU team, get direct answers to these:

- What is my expected role and realistic minutes on this roster?
- What tournaments and circuits will this team play, and who typically attends them?
- How does the coaching staff communicate with families about playing time?
- What's the team's actual competition level — not reputation, competition?

Vague answers to any of these are a warning sign.

## Shoe Circuits Aren't Magic

Nike EYBL, Under Armour, and Adidas circuit events do attract a concentration of college coaches. That's real. But it only matters if you're playing meaningful minutes in those games. A bench role on a circuit team is, from an exposure standpoint, often worse than a starting role on a strong regional program that plays similar-level competition without the shoe-brand attachment.

## Fit Changes as You Get Older

The right team at 14 or 15 is often not the right team at 16 or 17. Younger players benefit more from development-focused environments and heavy playing time. Older players in serious recruiting windows need to prioritize exposure at events where their target-level coaches are actually in the gym.

Reevaluate every offseason. Loyalty to a program that no longer serves your development or exposure needs isn't doing you any favors.

## The Verdict

Choose an AAU team based on realistic playing time, competition level, and coaching communication — not name recognition. A team that gets you real minutes against real competition will do more for your recruiting than a bench role on a famous roster.
