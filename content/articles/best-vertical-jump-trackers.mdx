Vertical jump wearables promise precise, session-long tracking that a manual vertec test simply can't provide. But accuracy varies wildly across devices. We benchmarked four popular trackers against manual video frame analysis across 50 standardized jumps per device.

## How We Tested

Each device recorded 50 jumps — a mix of standing vertical and approach jumps — performed by the same three testers. We cross-referenced every jump against high-frame-rate video analysis, considered the gold-standard manual measurement, and calculated the average error margin per device.

## Mount Position Matters More Than Brand

The clearest pattern in our data: hip-mounted sensors outperformed wrist-mounted and shoe-mounted sensors across every tester, regardless of brand. Wrist motion during a jump introduces enough noise to meaningfully affect accuracy, particularly for approach jumps that involve significant arm swing.

## The Verdict

The JumpMetric Pro was the standout performer in our test, combining the best accuracy with a genuinely useful companion app for tracking progress across a training block. If you're serious about quantifying jump training, it's worth the investment over cheaper wrist-based alternatives.
