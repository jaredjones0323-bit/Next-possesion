Most basketball shoe complaints — blisters, black toenails, "my feet fall asleep" — trace back to width, not length. Yet almost nobody measures foot width before buying. Here's how to actually do it.

## Measure Both Feet, Properly

Stand on a piece of paper with your full weight on one foot, trace the outline, and measure the widest point across the ball of the foot in millimeters. Repeat for the other foot — most people have a size difference between feet, and you should fit to the larger measurement.

## Brand Sizing Isn't Standardized

A "medium" width label means different things across brands. Two shoes labeled the same width from different manufacturers can differ by several millimeters at the forefoot. Always check a brand's specific width chart rather than assuming consistency across brands.

## The Thumb's-Width Test

With the shoe on and laced, you should be able to fit roughly a thumb's width between your longest toe and the front of the shoe, and your foot shouldn't feel pinched at the widest point of the forefoot. This is a fast sanity check but shouldn't replace measuring your actual foot width against a brand's chart.

## When to Size Up vs. Go Wide

If length is the issue, size up. If width is the issue, look for a wide-width version of the shoe rather than sizing up — a longer shoe with a heel that slips creates its own problems, including reduced court feel and blister risk at the heel.

## The Verdict

Ten minutes measuring your feet properly will save you from returns, blisters, and shoes that quietly hurt your performance. Measure both feet, check brand-specific width charts, and don't assume a size that worked in one brand will translate directly to another.
