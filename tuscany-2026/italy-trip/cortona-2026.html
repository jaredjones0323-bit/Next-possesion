ArriveMarket day

Florence → Cortona, arrive into the market

  • Leave Florence by 08:00. A1 south, ~1h45 to Cortona.
  • Park Parcheggio dello Spirito Santo (below the walls, escalators up).
  • Cortona Saturday market — Piazza Signorelli + Piazza Franciolini, approx. 07:00–13:00. Produce, cured meats, cheese, porchetta, flowers, housewares.
    • Eat a porchetta panino standing up. Non-negotiable.
    • Buy three days of villa food: pecorino, prosciutto, tomatoes, bread, peaches.
  • Enoteca Molesini — wine for the week. They deliver.
  • Conad, Camucia — bulk staples, water, coffee, on the drive to the villa.
  • Villa check-in (~16:00). Unpack slowly.
  • Fortezza del Girifalco at sunset — Val di Chiana and Lake Trasimeno.
  • Dinner: Trattoria Dardano. Wood grill, cheap, family-run, no ceremony. Right call for six tired adults on night one. may take walk-ins, but six is six
Why the market beats an Arezzo stop en route: the market runs only Saturdays, and your only two Saturdays are arrival and departure day. Arezzo keeps until Aug 28.

Lake day

Lake Trasimeno

Sunday: shops shut across Tuscany. The lake is the right move.

  • Drive to Passignano sul Trasimeno, ~35 min.
  • Ferry to Isola Maggiore — fishing village, lace-making tradition, lakeside lunch.
  • Afternoon: Castiglione del Lago — walk the castle ramparts, then swim.
  • Dinner: casual, back in Cortona or at the villa.
Confirm the last ferry back before boarding. Summer Sunday schedules are frequent but they do end.

Slow day

Cortona, deliberately unstructured

  • Nothing before 10:00.
  • MAEC (Etruscan Academy Museum) while it's still cool.
  • Basilica di Santa Margherita — opulent interior, 17th-c. bell tower, and the walk up is the point.
  • Long lunch. Riposo 14:00–17:00. Actually take it.
  • Aperitivo: La Vineria di Cortona — rooftop.
  • Dinner: La Bucaccia. Historic cellar setting, their own aging room, Chianina done properly. book ahead
Chianina. You are in Val di Chiana, the breed's home valley. The bistecca here is the real article, not a Florence-tourist-menu version. If you eat one great steak this week, eat it here.

Big day

Val d'Orcia loop

The big scenic day. One clean southwest arc — do not pair Val d'Orcia with Arezzo, they're in opposite directions.

  1. Montepulciano (early). Park below at San Donato — the climb is steep, don't fight the ZTL.
  2. Cantine Contucci or De' Ricci — underground cellars cut into the hill. confirm group size
  3. Pienza (~15 min) — Piazza Pio II, the cathedral, pecorino tasting.
  4. San Quirico d'Orcia — cypress roads, the classic Val d'Orcia views.
  5. Bagno Vignoni (late afternoon) — the village square is a thermal pool. Best possible end to a hot day.
  6. Home for a late dinner.
Optional swap: Bagni San Filippo / Fosso Bianco — free natural hot springs with white calcium formations, short forest walk. Wilder than Bagno Vignoni.

Siena

Siena

  • The Duomo floor is uncovered (18 Aug – 15 Nov 2026). 56 marble mosaic panels by ~40 artists, boarded over most of the year. This is the single best reason to time Siena now.
  • Buy the OPA SI Pass, not the basic cathedral ticket — it covers the Crypt, Baptistery, Museum, and Facciatone.
  • Add the Gate of Heaven rooftop walk. Small timed groups, sells out days ahead. book in advance
  • Bonus: Wednesday is La Lizza market morning.
  • Parking: San Francesco or Santa Caterina garages. Never drive into the center.
  • Dinner back in Cortona: Quanto Basta.

Slow dayMarket day

Slow day, local

  • Camucia market, Thursday morning — the town below Cortona. Entirely local, zero tourists. This is the anti-Cortona market.
  • Villa and pool all afternoon. Build lunch from La Botteghina (deli boards) and Market Via Dardano.
If anyone gets restless, two low-effort options:
  • Castiglion Fiorentino — 10 minutes. Almost nobody visits.
  • Lucignano — 30 minutes. A perfect elliptical medieval town, streets in concentric rings.

Arezzo + farewell

Arezzo half-day + farewell dinner

  • ~35 min north.
  • Piero della Francesca, Legend of the True Cross — Basilica di San Francesco. Timed entry, ~25 people per slot. This is the real reason to go and it's missing from most Tuscany lists. book ahead
  • Piazza Grande — the sloping square.
  • Note: Arezzo's famous antiques fair is the first weekend of the month. Aug 28 is not it. Go for the frescoes.
  • Back by 16:00.
  • Farewell dinner: L'Ortino di Cortona. Tasting menu, €100+. book now

Driving & parking

ZTL is the recurring hazard

Every historic center has a restricted traffic zone monitored by camera. Fines arrive at your rental company months after you're home. Rule: park below the walls, walk up. Always.

TownPark at
CortonaParcheggio dello Spirito Santo (escalators)
SienaSan Francesco or Santa Caterina garage
MontepulcianoSan Donato, at the bottom
ArezzoPietri or Eden (escalators up)
Montepulciano/PienzaLots outside the walls, signposted
Two-car logistics: designate one car as the wine car. Otherwise the Montepulciano crate ends up under six people's luggage.

The bench

Things to swap in

Only if the group wants more, not by default. The stated priority is slow time.

Water & heat relief

  • Bagni San Filippo (Fosso Bianco) — free natural hot springs, white calcium terraces
  • Bagno Vignoni — thermal village square
  • Castiglione del Lago — swim + castle

Untouristed small towns

  • Lucignano (30 min) — concentric elliptical street plan
  • Castiglion Fiorentino (10 min)
  • Anghiari (50 min) — steep, medieval, near Sansepolcro

Wine & food, hands-on

  • Avignonesi, Montepulciano — biodynamic estate, real tour, lunch on site. Best group option.
  • Cooking class at an agriturismo — six is the ideal size, you get the whole kitchen
  • Summer truffle hunt — scorzone is in season in August

Bigger days (would cost a slow day)

  • Montalcino + Brunello
  • Assisi or Perugia — ~1h, and Umbria is right there
